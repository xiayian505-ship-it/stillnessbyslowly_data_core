24 SOLAR TERMS REFERENCE
Version: 0.1-draft
Prepared: 2026-09-06

PURPOSE
=======
This file is the source/provenance and implementation contract for the future solar_terms.js module.
It is intended to be readable by both humans and AI-assisted development tools.

This file is NOT executable code.
Do not infer solar terms from lunar dates.
Do not silently change timezone conventions.
Do not hide known astronomical uncertainty.


SUPPORTED RANGE
===============
Target Gregorian range: 1901-01-01 through 2100-12-31.

The range is intentionally finite.
Dates outside this range are unsupported unless this reference is explicitly extended and re-verified.

Do not extrapolate beyond 1901-2100.


SCOPE
=====
The future solar_terms.js module may provide, within the supported range:

- The 24 solar terms for a Gregorian year
- Solar-term name
- Solar longitude associated with each term
- Civil date of a solar term for the selected Taiwan interpretation
- Solar-term instant/time when retained by the dataset
- Lookup by date
- Lookup of a named term in a year
- Next solar term after a date/time

Lunar-calendar conversion is NOT defined by this file.
See lunar_reference.txt.

Festival definitions are NOT defined by this file.


ASTRONOMICAL DEFINITION
=======================
The 24 solar terms are astronomical events defined by the apparent solar longitude,
spaced at 15-degree intervals around the ecliptic.

Examples:
- Vernal Equinox / 春分: 0 degrees
- 清明: 15 degrees
- Autumnal Equinox / 秋分: 180 degrees
- Winter Solstice / 冬至: 270 degrees

Solar terms must not be derived from lunar-calendar dates.


CANONICAL CIVIL-TIME INTERPRETATION
===================================
Intended consumer context:
Taiwan

Canonical modern offset for comparison:
UTC+8

When an astronomical source publishes in Japan Standard Time (JST / UTC+9),
subtract one hour to obtain the corresponding UTC+8 clock time.

IMPORTANT:
Historical civil-time rules and daylight-saving-time histories must not be casually
substituted between Hong Kong, Japan, and Taiwan.

HKO is primarily used here as an official published DATE reference across 1901-2100.
NAOJ is used as an independent astronomical calculation source.

Future implementation work that claims exact historical Asia/Taipei civil timestamps
must explicitly verify Taiwan's historical timezone rules rather than assuming that
all historical local clock times were permanently UTC+8.

INDEPENDENT ASTRONOMICAL SOURCE

Institution:

Calendar and Ephemeris Calculation Office

Tool:
二十四節気・雑節 長期版

Official URL:
https://eco.mtk.nao.ac.jp/cgi-bin/koyomi/cande/phenomena_sy.cgi

Output includes:
- Date
- Time
- Solar-term name
- Solar longitude

Published time basis:
Japan Standard Time / UT+9

NAOJ states that the long-term results are calculated using current theories and
parameters and may differ from previously published or future values.
The tool does not account for daylight-saving time.

Role:
Independent astronomical calculation and minute-level cross-check.

KNOWN ASTRONOMICAL BOUNDARY CASES
=================================
WARNING:

HKO states that calculations of moon phases and solar-term times many decades in the
future may have errors of several minutes. When an event is very close to midnight,
the published civil date can consequently differ by one day.

HKO specifically identifies solar-term date-risk cases including:

- 2051 Vernal Equinox / 春分
- 2083 Start of Spring / 立春
- 2084 Vernal Equinox / 春分

These are KNOWN BOUNDARY CASES, not ordinary software bugs.

Rules for these cases:

1. Preserve the selected authoritative dataset value.
2. Record the uncertainty.
3. Do not silently "correct" the date using another algorithm.
4. If authoritative sources or later revisions disagree, document the discrepancy.
5. A future AI modifying solar_terms.js must not remove the warning merely because
   another calculation returns a different civil date.


VERIFICATION POLICY
===================
Before finalizing the production dataset:

1. Compare HKO published solar-term dates against an independent astronomical source.
2. Convert timezones explicitly.
3. Test all 24 terms in representative years, not only equinoxes and solstices.
4. Give extra attention to events close to 00:00 local civil time.
5. Preserve source institution, URL, retrieval date, and calculation convention.
6. Record known disagreements and revisions.
7. Do not use a single algorithm as both implementation and sole verification source.


IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT FOR FUTURE solar_terms.js
==================================================
The future module should:

- Reject unsupported dates explicitly.
- Never extrapolate beyond the verified supported range.
- Keep solar-term logic independent from lunar-calendar conversion.
- Use explicit timezone/civil-date rules.
- Preserve known boundary-case warnings in documentation.
- Avoid claiming false astronomical precision.
- Expose supported-range information to consumers if practical.

A future AI or developer modifying solar_terms.js should read this reference first.
